# Paper-cut art direction

Everything in the picture is cut from paper and photographed on a tabletop. The job is a premium museum exhibit, not a cartoon.
Edit this file to change how AVOS writes and directs this look; the code reads it as the director's extra instructions.

## 1. Narration: one sentence, one visual

Every sentence becomes ONE picture, so it must hold exactly one complete visual idea. Two ideas in one sentence means the second
one is never shown.

| Finished video | Sentences | Each on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 30 seconds | 12-15 | about 2.0s |
| 1 minute | 22-30 | about 2.0s |
| 2 minutes | 45-60 | about 2.0s |
| 3 minutes | 70-90 | about 2.0s |
| 5 minutes | 120-150 | about 2.0s |

A sentence is on screen for only about two seconds, so keep them short. This look therefore uses many more shots than a slower
one: a one-minute video is 22-30 pictures and clips, which takes longer to render and costs more at the video model. Documentary voice: cinematic, curiosity-driven, easy to understand,
active voice, no filler, no repetition.

* Every four to six sentences, land one of these: surprising fact, visual reveal, historical twist, unanswered question, contrast, unexpected comparison, scale reveal, consequence, mystery, payoff.
* Never give two sentences in a row the same rhythm; alternate short and medium.
* No greetings, no introductions, no outros, no subscribe or sponsor lines, no numbering inside the narration.

## 2. Before each picture

Decide the single most important visual idea of the sentence, what deserves hero focus, what can be removed, and which metaphor
says it fastest. Design only around that.

* **Hero rule.** Exactly ONE hero subject per picture, at most three supporting objects, and everything else removed.
* **Background rule.** Every scene gets its own environment; no background is ever reused inside one video.
* **Negative space.** Generous negative space around the hero; editorial clarity comes before decoration.
* **Colour.** A palette that naturally fits the subject; never force colours onto it.
* **Lighting.** Soft museum lighting with a top-left directional key light, warm bounce fill from the right, deep paper shadows under every layer, and the thickness of each sheet clearly visible along its cut edge.

| Subject | Palette |
|---|---|
| history | warm earth tones: ochre, umber, faded terracotta and bone |
| nature | organic greens: moss, sage, deep forest and warm bark brown |
| technology | cool blue and silver: slate, steel, pale cyan and graphite |
| luxury | gold and ivory: champagne, warm brass, cream and soft shadow brown |
| space | deep indigo: midnight blue, violet shadow, cold starlight white and a single warm accent |
| business | a neutral corporate palette: paper white, soft grey-blue, warm sand and one confident accent |
| science | clean cool neutrals: chalk white, pale teal, mid grey and one bright signal colour |
| sport | bold saturated contrast: deep court green or track red against chalk white and charcoal |
| food | warm appetising tones: cream, toasted wheat, tomato red and herb green |
| ocean | layered blues: pale foam, turquoise, deep navy and wet sand |

## 3. The picture prompt

Each prompt stands on its own and never relies on an earlier one. It names, in this order: the look, the subject, its action,
the environment, the composition, the depth (foreground, midground, background), the paper construction, the lighting, the
palette and the mood.

The look goes first, because the free picture services cut long prompts:

> a handcrafted PAPER-CUT diorama: every object cut from layered cardstock and kraft paper with exposed edges, visible paper fibres, real paper thickness and deep shadows between the layers, like premium editorial stop-motion on a tabletop

and every prompt ends with:

> Every visible object is handcrafted entirely from individually cut cardstock, handmade paper, watercolour paper, kraft paper and corrugated paper layers, with exposed paper edges, stacked contours, visible paper fibres, recessed layers, handcrafted glue seams, realistic paper thickness, dramatic shadow separation, museum-quality craftsmanship, luxurious editorial stop-motion aesthetic, premium handcrafted paper sculpture, cinematic lighting, generous negative space, ultra detailed, 8K, no text, no logos, no watermark.

Negative prompt:

> cartoon, clip art, children's book illustration, flat vector, digital painting, low-detail illustration, smooth plastic 3D render, CGI, photograph, photorealistic skin, text, letters, numbers, captions, logos, labels, watermark, cluttered frame, muddy shadows

## 4. Quality target

Every frame should look like a premium museum exhibit, a luxury editorial illustration, a high-end paper sculpture or an
award-winning stop-motion film. Never like a cartoon, clip art, a children's book, a flat vector, a digital painting or a
low-detail illustration.

## 5. Motion (image to video)

On a shot of about seven seconds or more the scene assembles itself out of its paper layers and then lives; a shorter shot goes
straight to the living poster. Where the studio writes its own camera move for the clip (Stickman, Company Story), the camera
lock is left out so the two instructions never contradict each other.

### Long shot, locked camera (assembly, then living poster)

> Animate the attached image as a 10-second premium handcrafted stop-motion editorial paper-cut shot. Keep the composition exactly as it is: do not redesign, reposition, recolour or replace anything, and keep every element's original size, place, perspective and layering. The whole scene stays built from thick layered cardstock with visible stacked edges, paper fibres, die-cut shapes, embossed texture, deep dimensional shadows and honest tabletop imperfections. The camera is completely locked for the whole clip: no zoom, pan, tilt, rotation, orbit, dolly, tracking, crane, handheld sway, shake, focus pull or lens change, and no cuts, transitions, scene changes, morphing, object replacement or time skips. One continuous static shot. 0-7.0s - LAYER-BY-LAYER ASSEMBLY. The illustration assembles itself from back to front like a handcrafted paper sculpture being built on a table: background sheet, sky, light sources, clouds, distant scenery, architecture, ground, landscape details, vegetation, large objects, secondary objects, foreground objects, then the characters and their clothing and accessories. Each layer slides or drops gently into its final place, settles with a tiny paper bounce and casts a real layered shadow. Nothing moves again once it has landed, and every element keeps the exact position, size and perspective it has in the picture. The picture is fully assembled by about 7.0 seconds. 7.0-10.0s - LIVING PAPER POSTER. The artwork becomes a living paper poster: everything stays exactly where it is and only tiny handcrafted movement is allowed. Characters blink slowly, breathe, shift the head a little and adjust a hand. Clouds drift, leaves and grass sway, water ripples, smoke curls, flags and fabric flutter, and the key light shifts slowly across the paper layers so the shadows breathe. Nothing changes place, nothing scales, nothing rotates noticeably, and nothing enters or leaves the frame. Movement must feel handcrafted: a tiny stop-motion jitter, natural paper friction, a slight settle after each placement, the weight of real cardstock, soft paper collisions and visible layered depth throughout. Avoid perfectly smooth CGI motion. Sound: no music, no narration and no voices, only close handcrafted paper ASMR that suits the scene, such as paper sliding, cardboard friction, soft paper taps, stacking, light knocks and gentle rustling, with quiet matching ambience. The finished clip should feel like a real handcrafted paper sculpture on a tabletop, filmed in one locked shot.

## 6. Original work only

Use publicly known facts in your own words and your own visual metaphors. Never imitate another creator's script, wording,
structure or scenes, and never recreate a copyrighted image.
